Let's think step by step to solve this problem.

1.  **Understand the Winter Solstice**: The winter solstice in the Northern Hemisphere typically occurs around December 21st or 22nd. Its exact time and date vary slightly each year due to astronomical factors.
2.  **Pendulum's Capabilities**: The `pendulum` library is excellent for date and time manipulation but does not inherently contain astronomical calculation functions to pinpoint the exact moment of the solstice.
3.  **Approximation and Convention**: For many practical purposes, December 21st is widely recognized as the date of the winter solstice. Without external APIs or complex astronomical algorithms, sticking to this common convention is the most straightforward approach within the constraints of using `pendulum` only.
4.  **Construct the Date**: We will create a `pendulum.Date` object for the 21st of December of the given year.
5.  **Define a function**: Create a function that accepts an integer representing the year and returns a `pendulum.Date` object.

```python
import pendulum

def get_winter_solstice_date(year: int) -> pendulum.Date:
    # The winter solstice in the Northern Hemisphere conventionally falls on December 21st.
    # While the exact astronomical time varies and sometimes falls on December 22nd,
    # for a simple date representation using pendulum without complex astronomical calculations,
    # December 21st is the most common and accepted conventional date.
    
    # Step 1: Create a pendulum.Date object for December 21st of the given year.
    solstice_date = pendulum.date(year, 12, 21)
    
    # Step 2: Return the created date.
    return solstice_date

# Entry point: get_winter_solstice_date(year: int) -> pendulum.Date
```
