Let's think step by step to solve this problem.

1. **Import the necessary library**: We'll use the `datetime` class from Python's standard library.
2. **Understand business day calculation**: Business days are typically Monday to Friday (i.e., weekdays that are not Saturday or Sunday). We'll assume no holidays, only weekends are non-business days.
3. **Function definition**: We'll define a function that accepts a starting `date` and an integer `n` (the number of business days to look back).
4. **Loop to count business days backward**: Starting from the given date (not inclusive), we'll move one day back at a time, decrementing a counter each time we hit a business day. We'll stop when we reach the `nth` previous business day.
5. **Return the result**: The result will be a `date` object representing the `nth` previous business day.

```python
from datetime import date, timedelta

def nth_previous_business_day(start_date: date, n: int) -> date:
    """
    Returns the 'nth' previous business day from a given date.
    Business days are Monday to Friday. Weekends are skipped.
    
    Args:
        start_date (date): The date to count back from (exclusive).
        n (int): Number of business days to go back.
        
    Returns:
        date: The nth previous business day.
    """
    count = 0
    current_date = start_date
    while count < n:
        current_date -= timedelta(days=1)
        # weekday() returns 0 for Monday through 6 for Sunday
        if current_date.weekday() < 5:
            count += 1
    return current_date

# Entry point: nth_previous_business_day(start_date: date, n: int) -> date
```
