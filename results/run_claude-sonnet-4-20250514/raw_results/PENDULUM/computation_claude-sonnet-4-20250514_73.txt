Let's think step by step to solve this problem.

1. **Import the necessary libraries**: We need the pendulum library to work with dates and times.
2. **Define a function**: We will create a function that accepts a pendulum.DateTime object as input.
3. **Extract the time component**: From the DateTime object, we need to get just the time portion to compare against business hours.
4. **Define business hours**: Create time objects for 9 AM and 5 PM to represent the start and end of business hours.
5. **Compare the time**: Check if the given time falls between 9 AM and 5 PM (assuming 5 PM is exclusive).
6. **Return the result**: Return a boolean indicating whether the time is within business hours.

```python
import pendulum

def is_within_business_hours(dt: pendulum.DateTime) -> bool:
    # Step 1: Extract the time portion from the datetime
    time_part = dt.time()
    
    # Step 2: Define business hours (9 AM to 5 PM)
    business_start = pendulum.time(9, 0, 0)  # 9:00 AM
    business_end = pendulum.time(17, 0, 0)   # 5:00 PM (17:00 in 24-hour format)
    
    # Step 3: Check if the time falls within business hours
    # Business hours are from 9 AM (inclusive) to 5 PM (exclusive)
    return business_start <= time_part < business_end

# Entry point: is_within_business_hours(dt: pendulum.DateTime) -> bool
```
