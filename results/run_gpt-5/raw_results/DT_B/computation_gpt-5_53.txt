Let's think step by step to solve this problem.
1. Choose a reliable civil-date approximation for the September (autumnal) equinox. A widely used empirical formula (used for Japanese holiday calculations) estimates the day-of-month as floor(23.2488 + 0.242194*(year - 1980) - floor((year - 1980)/4)).
2. Restrict the valid range to years where the approximation is accurate for civil dates (1900–2099).
3. Compute the day-of-month using the formula and construct a date object for September of the given year.
4. Return the resulting date.

```python
from datetime import date

def autumnal_equinox_date(year: int) -> date:
    """
    Estimate the date of the September (autumnal) equinox for a given Gregorian year.
    Valid for years 1900 through 2099 (civil-date approximation).
    
    Parameters:
        year (int): Gregorian calendar year.
    
    Returns:
        date: Estimated date of the September equinox in the given year.
    """
    if year < 1900 or year > 2099:
        raise ValueError("Year out of supported range: 1900–2099")

    y = year - 1980
    # Empirical civil-date formula used for Japanese holiday calculations
    day = int((23.2488 + 0.242194 * y) - int(y / 4))
    return date(year, 9, day)
```
